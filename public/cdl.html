How would you teach a brand new student to shift?

  • Safety first: empty lot, parking brake on, switch seats.
  • Neutral, start, first gear. Release the clutch to find the catch point.
  • Clutch in, shift to second, clutch out, slow.
  • Explain why gears grind, then have them repeat it back before practicing.
  • On a 10 speed: watch RPMs, listen to the engine, double clutch, learn the gear pattern.

How would you teach a nervous student to back up?

  • Ask why they think it's happening.
  • New students: I straighten the wheel. More experienced: they fix it themselves.
  • Small movements, wait a couple of seconds, watch the trailer react.
  • Set up an angle and arc into the spot, don't come in straight.
  • Get out and look when unsure.

How would you manage a class of eight with one truck?

  • Find each student's weak points and work on them.
  • Put stronger students to work helping the others.
  • Keep waiting students busy: practice pre-trip out loud on a parked truck, or quiz each other.

A student fails their pre-trip and wants to quit. What do you do?

  • "Rome wasn't built in a day. I failed my test twice myself."
  • Go over what they missed, then have them do the same order every time.
  • Redo it right away so they leave with a win.

An overconfident student ignores your corrections. What do you do?

A serious safety mistake happens. What do you do?

  • Stop the truck calmly, no yelling.
  • Explain why it was dangerous and what could have happened.
  • Have them redo it correctly.
  • Note it in their file.

How would you teach a student to couple to a trailer?

  • Inspect the fifth wheel, chock the trailer, line up, back until the fifth wheel just touches.
  • Check trailer height, connect air lines, supply air, lock trailer brakes.
  • Back under, then do the tug test by pulling gently forward with the trailer brakes locked.
  • Get out and look: jaws closed around the kingpin, no gap. Connect electrical, raise landing gear.

How would you teach the air brake test?

  • Know every gauge and what it tells you, especially the air pressure gauges.
  • Build full pressure, shut off the engine, release the parking brake, watch for air loss.
  • Have the student point and explain each step back to you. Don't let them go through the motions.

What is a high hook?

  • "The trailer is too high when you back under it, so the fifth wheel rides up on top of the trailer plate instead of sliding under. The kingpin never locks into the jaws."
  • It can look coupled but isn't. That's why we do the tug test and get out and look.

Air lines: which is which?

RED = EMERGENCY. Supplies air to the trailer tanks and holds the spring brakes off.
BLUE = SERVICE. Carries the signal to apply trailer brakes when you press the pedal.
